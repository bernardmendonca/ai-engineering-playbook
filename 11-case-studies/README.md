# 10 — Case Studies

> Real-world patterns of AI adoption in engineering organizations — what worked, what didn't, and lessons learned.

## Why This Section Exists

Theory is useful but incomplete. Engineering leaders need to see how organizations at their scale navigated AI adoption — what worked, what didn't, and what they'd do differently. Without this:

- Every organization reinvents the same mistakes (big-bang rollouts, skipped baselines, ignored resistance)
- Leaders lack calibration on realistic timelines, costs, and outcomes
- Context-free advice ("just use AI!") doesn't account for regulatory constraints, team size, or culture
- Success and failure patterns remain tribal knowledge instead of documented, shareable insight

These case studies provide that calibration — composite examples based on real adoption patterns, organized so you can find the scenario closest to yours.

## How to Use This Section

These case studies are composite examples based on common adoption patterns observed across the industry. They're not attributed to specific companies but reflect real scenarios engineering leaders encounter.

Each case study follows this structure:
1. **Context** — Org size, industry, starting point
2. **Challenge** — What problem they were solving
3. **Approach** — What they did
4. **Results** — Quantitative and qualitative outcomes
5. **Lessons** — What they'd do differently
6. **Applicable if** — When this pattern fits your situation

## Patterns by Outcome

### What Worked

| Pattern | Case Study | Key success factor |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Champions-led grassroots adoption | Mid-Size | Peer influence > mandates |
| Security-first, then speed | Enterprise | Cleared blockers before scaling |
| Measurement from day one | All | Data-driven decisions at every stage |
| Phased rollout with checkpoints | Mid-Size, Enterprise | Built confidence gradually |
| Free-tier exploration before budget | All | Leaders had hands-on experience |

### What Didn't Work

| Pattern | Case Study | Why it failed |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| Big-bang org-wide rollout | Enterprise (first attempt) | Overwhelmed support, inconsistent adoption |
| Mandated usage metrics | Mid-Size (abandoned) | Gaming, resentment, meaningless data |
| No baseline metrics before pilot | Startup | Couldn't prove improvement to investors |
| Ignoring security review | Startup (caught early) | Almost exposed customer data |
| Choosing tool by hype, not fit | Mid-Size (first attempt) | Wrong tool for their ecosystem |
